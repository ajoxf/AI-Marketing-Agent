# Broker and platform content licensing + exit options

Status: **proposal.** Prices are starting points. The flat revenue split applies to all of
it. Nothing here changes the live site.

## Part A: Broker and platform licensing

### 1. What brokers are actually buying

Brokers don't buy research because it's good. They buy it because it **keeps clients
active and trading**, gives their marketing something to send, and fills their education
hub. Trading Central, the market leader, is paid by brokers and given free to their
clients, and many brokers display it.
([ForexBrokers.com](https://www.forexbrokers.com/guides/trading-central-brokers))
Autochartist describes its product as "client engagement automation through market
data-driven content".
([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/onezero-acquires-autochartist-302378119.html))

**Your pitch:** *"Your clients already get algorithmic pattern alerts. Give them analysis
from credentialed humans: CMT charterholders, with a weekly outlook, live webinars, and an
academy, under your brand."*

That's the gap. Most broker content is automated or generic; yours is named experts with
a professional designation.

### 2. White-label or not? Offer three tiers and lead with co-branded

| Model | What the client sees | Pros | Cons | Price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Branded** | "Research by NordStar Pro" inside the broker's portal (how Trading Central appears) | Builds your brand and B2C pipeline; cheapest for the broker | Some brokers don't want another brand in front of their clients | Lowest (Starter) |
| **Co-branded (default)** | "[Broker] Market Insights, powered by NordStar Pro", with expert bylines | Broker owns the experience; you keep credit and the experts keep their names | — | Standard |
| **Full white-label** | Broker's brand only | What large brokers sometimes require | You build no brand equity; an acquirer values you less; expert bylines may be dropped | **Premium: about 2× co-branded**, enterprise only |

**Recommendation:**
- **Default to co-branded.** Sell full white-label only to large brokers, at a premium, on
  a 12+ month term.
- Even in white-label, **keep author names and credentials on each piece.** Credentialed
  bylines are the product, and regulators expect research to identify its author.
- **Expert contracts must allow it.** They must let NordStar Pro license the experts'
  work, including white-label with attribution. Add this clause before the first pilot.

### 3. The product: "NordStar Pro Broker Suite"

| Component | What | Format | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Market Outlook** | Weekly outlooks on the broker's instruments (FX majors, indices, gold, oil, crypto) | HTML widget/iframe, PDF, email-ready newsletter block | NordStar Pro experts |
| **Daily Levels** (needed by most brokers) | Short daily note: key levels, what would change the view | Same formats | NordStar Pro experts. **New workload: confirm expert capacity before selling it** |
| **Live webinars** | 1–2 per month by a CMT expert, for the broker's clients | Zoom/YouTube, broker-branded | Experts |
| **Trading Academy** | Fincoursa technical-analysis modules in the broker's education hub | Hosted access with broker branding, or embed | Fincoursa instructors |
| **Marketing pack** | Monthly SEO articles and social posts the broker can publish | Docs + images | Agent drafts, experts approve |

**Frequency is the main product risk.** Brokers are used to daily or intraday content. A
weekly-only feed sells at Starter level at most. Decide whether your experts can deliver a
daily short note before month 3.

### 4. Pricing (proposal)

| Tier | Includes | Price | Term |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Starter** (branded) | Weekly outlook, 1 webinar/month, 1 language | $3,000/month | 12 months |
| **Growth** (co-branded) | Weekly + daily levels, 2 webinars/month, Academy (up to N clients), marketing pack | $6,000–8,000/month | 12 months |
| **Enterprise** (white-label option) | Everything + custom instruments, extra languages, feed/API, white-label | From $12,000/month | 12–36 months |
| Setup fee | Branding, instrument list, integration | $2,500–5,000 one-time | — |
| **Paid pilot** | 3 months of Starter or Growth, credited on conversion | $4,500 | 3 months |

**Avoid revenue share or per-client pricing** at the start. It's hard to audit, and it
ties your income to the broker's marketing. Flat monthly fees are simpler and easier to
value at exit.

**Revenue split (flat rule, no exceptions):**
- The order form lists each component's price.
- **Research components** (outlook, daily levels, webinars, marketing pack): experts get
  **70% of net**, split equally across contributing experts.
- **Academy component:** instructors get **50% of net**, split equally across the
  included modules.

### 5. Who to target

| Priority | Type | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Regulated mid-size brokers that already buy content** (they show Trading Central or Autochartist) | Budget already exists; pitch as an addition ("human, credentialed analysis") or replacement |
| 2 | **Futures and options brokers** (NFA/FINRA regulated) | Technical analysis and derivatives are your experts' strengths |
| 3 | **Trading platforms and charting tools** | Need content to keep users engaged; may integrate it natively |
| 4 | **Licensed crypto platforms** | Hungry for credible analysis; check licences carefully |
| 5 | **Neobroker and fintech investing apps** | Education focus; fits the Fincoursa Academy |

**Exclusions** (from the B2B recommendations): unregulated or offshore brokers, binary
options, aggressive CFD shops, unlicensed crypto, retail funded-trader challenge firms, and
anyone wanting trade buttons, copy-trading, or "signals" attached to the research.

### 6. How to sell it (months 3–12)

| Month | Do |
|---|---|
| **2–3** | Build the **sample feed**: 4 weeks of outlooks and daily levels on a demo page styled as a fictional broker, plus 1 recorded webinar and 1 Academy module. Agent builds a list of 40 target brokers: who already buys content, who runs the education hub, which regulator. Lawyer reviews the licence agreement. |
| **3–4** | 1:1 outreach to the **head of education or head of marketing** (decision makers) at 40 brokers, with experts' credentials up front. Book demos. Attend one broker industry event (iFX EXPO or a Finance Magnates summit) as a visitor to meet heads of marketing. |
| **4–6** | 15–20 demos → 3 paid pilots. **Agree success metrics up front:** content views, webinar attendance, client logins, and the broker's own activation or retention numbers. |
| **6–9** | Pilot reviews → first 1–2 annual contracts. Write case studies with the numbers. |
| **9–12** | Use case studies to sign brokers 3–5. Approach trading-platform vendors about a **distribution partnership** (their marketplace or content add-on reaches hundreds of brokers at once). |

**Target by month 12:** 3–5 brokers at about $5K/month average, roughly **$180–300K a
year in contracted revenue**.

### 7. Delivery and compliance

- **Start manual:** deliver the first 1–2 clients by email (PDF + HTML snippet) and a
  hosted page. Build a proper feed, widget, or API **only after 2 contracts are signed**.
  Build it as a separate service, so the live NordStar Pro site stays frozen.
- **Service levels:** publish times (e.g. daily note by 07:00 London), a corrections
  policy, and a backup author for each instrument.
- **Compliance:**
  - Every piece carries the author, credentials, date and time, conflicts disclosure, and
    risk warning.
  - The broker adds its own regulatory wording and is responsible for distribution to its
    clients (EU/UK marketing-communication rules, FINRA rules for US broker-dealers
    distributing third-party research).
  - The agreement states this.
  - Never write "buy/sell now" language. Write levels, scenarios, and invalidation.
- **Legal opinion first:** the regulatory questions in `b2b-plan.md` §10.5 must be
  answered before the first pilot, because brokers will ask on the first call.

|---|---|---|---|

| **3. Education roll-up / private equity** | PE-backed training platforms | **Wall Street Prep (Riverside-owned) bought Financial Edge and Euromoney Learning (Oct 2025)** ([Riverside](https://www.riversidecompany.com/currents/wall-street-prep-add-ons-news-release)) | Fincoursa: courses, certificate, university and B2B training contracts |
| **4. A broker or exchange** | A large broker wanting in-house research and education | — | Team + content; often priced lower |
| **5. No sale** | Keep it as a cash-flow business, or sell a minority stake | — | — |

**Combined or separate:**
- **Together:** if broker contracts bundle research and academy, sell the two businesses
  together as one "research + education for brokers and professionals" company. It's
  worth more combined to buyers 1 and 3.
- **Separately:** if the B2B revenue stays separate, sell Fincoursa to an education
  buyer and NordStar Pro to a broker-tech buyer.

### 9. What makes it sellable (start now; it's cheap now and expensive later)

1. **Own the rights.** The platform needs an **exclusive, perpetual, assignable** licence
   to experts' published content and its use in B2B and white-label products. Buyers walk
   away from marketplaces where the supply can leave and take the archive with them.
   **This is the most important item on this list.**
2. **Assignable contracts.** Broker, B2B, and expert contracts must survive a change of
   control (assignment clauses).
3. **No key-person dependence.** Many experts, a house brand, a backup author for every
   instrument, and notice periods in expert contracts.
4. **Recurring, contracted revenue.** Annual or multi-year B2B contracts, with the share
   of revenue that is contracted rather than monthly B2C reported every month.
5. **No customer concentration.** No single broker above about 25% of revenue.
6. **Clean records.** Separate books per business, revenue recognised properly, monthly
   expert and affiliate statements, and a clean compliance history (complaints log,
   corrections log, legal opinion on file).
7. **Metrics buyers ask for:** net revenue retention, gross margin, churn, CAC payback, and
   broker engagement data.

### 10. What it could be worth (rules of thumb, not a valuation)

- **Buyers value your take, not gross sales.** Under the flat split, the platform keeps 30%
  of research revenue and 50% of course revenue. A buyer values that revenue and the
  profit it produces, not the gross amount customers pay.
- **Typical ranges, which vary widely by market:**
  - Small content and education businesses usually sell on a **multiple of profit**,
    roughly 3–6×.
  - Businesses with mostly **recurring, contracted B2B revenue** and growth can be priced
    on a **multiple of revenue** instead.
  - Strategic buyers can pay more when your contracts fill a gap for them.
  - Get an M&A adviser's view when the time comes; these are not quotes.
- **Realistic timing:** 3–5+ years. Buyers want several years of financials, B2B renewal
  history, and proof the business runs without the founder.

### 11. Decisions needed

1. Can the experts commit to a **daily** short note? This decides whether you can sell
   above Starter.
2. Approve the co-branded default, with white-label only at a premium.
3. Approve adding the **rights and assignment clauses** to expert and instructor
   contracts now, via your lawyer.
4. Long-term: build to sell (prioritise contracted B2B revenue and rights) or build for
   cash flow? It changes what to optimise from year one.
